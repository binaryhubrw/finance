01 What this system is for

The hub takes money in and pays money out. This system records every franc of it, so that at any moment two questions have a clear answer:

The two questions

How much money does the hub have?

Who decided each entry was correct?

It works the way a paper cashbook works, with one rule added: two different people are involved in every entry. One person records what happened. A different person checks it and approves it. Until that second person approves, the entry sits in the book but does not count towards the balance.

That is the whole idea. Everything else in this manual follows from it. Amounts are in Rwandan francs.

02 The four roles

Your role decides what you can do. An administrator sets it when your account is created.

Role What it is for Can do
Accountant The person who handles the money day to day Records entries. Edits or cancels their own entries, but only while still pending. Views the ledger and reports. Exports CSV.
Auditor The person who checks the accountant's work Approves, rejects and voids entries. Reads the audit trail and exceptions. Closes months. Exports. Cannot record or edit entries.
Administrator The person who runs the system Everything an auditor can do, plus manages people and budget lines.
Viewer Anyone who needs to see the numbers Reads the ledger and reports. Changes nothing.

Why an auditor cannot record entries

This looks like a missing feature and is not. If the same person could both write an entry and approve it, the check would be worthless. The system enforces the split on the server, not by hiding buttons — the answer is the same whoever asks and however they ask.

For the same reason, nobody can approve an entry they recorded themselves, including an administrator. If you try:

You recorded this entry, so someone else has to approve it. Ask another auditor to review it.

03 Five rules that govern everything

Learn these and the rest of the system explains itself.

Approval gates the balance

A new entry is pending. It appears in the ledger and you can see it, but it does not move the running balance and does not appear in reports. Only after an auditor approves it does it count. This is why the balance column shows a small dash, not a number, next to a pending row.

Nothing is ever deleted

There is no delete button anywhere, and no hidden one either. An entry that was a mistake gets voided — it stays visible, dimmed, struck through, with the reason shown beside it. The record of the mistake is part of the record.

Editing after review undoes the review

If an approved or rejected entry is edited, the earlier decision no longer applies to what the entry now says. So it goes back to pending, the reviewer is cleared, and it is flagged as edited. It reappears in the approvals queue marked "re-submitted after edit", and the auditor is told exactly what changed.

Closed months are frozen

At the end of a month an auditor closes it. After that, nothing dated inside that month can be created, edited, voided or approved. A month cannot be closed while any entry inside it is still waiting for approval.

March 2026 is closed. Record the correction in an open month instead.

Every action is written down

Recording, editing, approving, rejecting, voiding, closing a month, adding a person, even signing in — all of it goes to the audit trail with who did it, when, and exactly which fields changed. The trail is append-only. Nobody can edit or delete it, including administrators.

04 The life of an entry

  • Pending Recorded, waiting. Does not count.
  • Approved Checked and accepted. Counts.
  • Rejected Refused with a reason. Stays visible.
  • Void Cancelled with a reason. Struck through.

05 Getting started

Signing in

Go to the address your administrator gave you and enter your email and password. After ten failed attempts the system pauses sign-in for that account for fifteen minutes.

Change your password on the first day

Your first password was typed by whoever created your account, so they know it. Open Settings → Your account and change it. You will need your current password and a new one of at least 8 characters.

If you have forgotten your password, an administrator can set a new one from Settings → People → Reset password. There is no email reset.

06 Screen by screen

Overview

Where you land when you sign in. Pick a month at the top; it opens on the current month. Four figures run across it: opening balance (what the hub held at the start of the month), money in and money out for the month, and closing balance — the headline number, what the hub holds now.

Then Awaiting approval, showing how many entries are waiting and what they are worth; click it to go straight to Approvals. Below that, six months of money in and out side by side, and the five largest expenses of the month. Every figure here counts approved entries only.

Ledger

The cashbook, and the screen you will use most.

Columns run Date · Description · Method · Status · Money in · Money out · Balance, with the payee, reference and budget line under each description.

Reading the rows

  • The row above the first entry is the opening balance — what was already in the book before this range starts.
  • Pending rows carry an amber marker down the left edge, and the balance column shows a dash. That gap is the point: the entry has not counted yet.
  • Void rows are dimmed and struck through, with the reason shown.
  • The Balance column sits in its own tinted lane, separated by a rule — the ruled balance column of a physical cashbook. It only ever counts approved entries.

The balance column disappears when you filter

A running balance only makes sense over a continuous stretch of the book. The moment you filter by budget line, status, person or search term you are looking at a selection, and a running total over a selection would be misleading. Filter by date alone and the column comes back.

Export CSV downloads exactly what you are looking at, with amounts as plain numbers so a spreadsheet adds them up correctly.

Recording an entry

Accountant Administrator

Record entry on the Ledger opens the form.

FieldWhat to put in it
DateThe day the money actually moved, not the day you are typing. Future dates are refused.
DirectionMoney in or money out.
AmountDigits only.
Budget lineThe list narrows to lines matching the direction — income lines for money in, expense lines for money out.
DescriptionWhat it was for, in plain words. This is what the auditor reads.
Paid to / Received fromThe other party.
MethodCash, mobile money, bank or other.
ReferenceReceipt number, MoMo transaction ID, cheque number.
Receipt linkA link to a photo of the receipt — a Drive or Photos link is fine. The system stores the link, not the image.
NoteAnything the auditor should know.

Fill in the reference or the receipt link for every expense. An expense with neither is flagged under Exceptions, because months later there is no way to verify it happened. When you save, the entry is pending and the screen says so. It is now the auditor's turn.

Fixing a mistake. While an entry is still pending you can edit your own entries freely. Once it has been approved or rejected, editing it sends it back to pending for a fresh decision. You can only touch entries you recorded yourself.

Importing a spreadsheet

Accountant Administrator

Import a sheet sits next to Record entry. Use it when you have a batch to enter — a month of receipts, a list from the bank.

  1. Download the template. It opens in Excel with the right columns already.
  2. Fill in one row per entry. Dates as dd/mm/yyyy, budget lines spelled the way they appear in the system. The dialog lists every line in use, so copy from there.
  3. Upload it. .xlsx straight from Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets works, so does .csv. Up to 2MB and 500 rows at a time.
  4. Check the preview. Nothing has been saved yet. Every row is listed either as ready or with the exact problem — a budget line that does not exist, a date in the future, an amount that is not a number.
  5. Import. The button turns on only when every row is ready.

A sheet with any bad row imports nothing

This is on purpose. Half a batch in the book, with no easy way to tell which half, is worse than a rejected file. Fix the rows it names and upload again. Imported entries are pending exactly like typed ones, and each gets its own line in the audit trail naming the file it came from.

Approvals

Auditor Administrator

The auditor's working screen. Oldest first, because the oldest have waited longest. Each entry shows everything you need to decide without clicking anywhere: description, amount, budget line, who recorded it and when, and the receipt reference. Warning lines appear when something deserves attention — no receipt reference, or the entry was edited after a previous review.

  • Approve — the entry counts from that moment, and the balance moves by exactly that amount.
  • Reject — you must give a reason. The entry stays visible with your reason attached, so the accountant knows what to fix.

When the queue is empty it says "Nothing waiting. The accountant is up to date."

Void, not reject, when an entry should never have existed at all — a duplicate, a payment that was reversed. Voiding also needs a reason. The entry stays in the book, struck through, and stops counting.

Audit trail

Everything that has happened, newest first. Each line shows the time, who did it, what they did and which entry. Changes are spelled out field by field — amount: 40,000 → 45,000. Snapshot on any row opens the full record as it stood right after that action.

Filter by person, action or date to answer a specific question: what did this person change last Tuesday, show me every void this quarter.

This screen is read-only for everyone. There is no way to alter the trail from anywhere in the system, and no administrator setting that unlocks it.

Exceptions

The screen an auditor should open first. It does not find fraud — it finds the entries most worth a second look, in six lists.

ListWhy it matters
Missing receiptAn expense with no receipt reference cannot be verified later.
Edited after reviewThese changed after someone had already reviewed them, so the earlier decision no longer applies.
Large amountsAnything at or above the threshold deserves a second signature.
BackdatedRecorded more than seven days after the date on the entry, so the paperwork trail may be thin.
Possible duplicatesEntries sharing a date, an amount and a budget line are often the same payment recorded twice.
Waiting too longPending for more than seven days. The balance is understated until these are cleared.

Every row links into the ledger so you can see the entry in context, and empty sections collapse to a quiet "Nothing flagged". A flagged entry is not necessarily wrong — ask about it, and if the answer is satisfactory, note it and move on.

Reports

Totals over any date range, with shortcuts for This month, Last month, This quarter and This year. You get summary figures, spending by budget line as bars with each line's share — and budget against actual where a monthly budget is set — income by budget line, the split by payment method, and a month-by-month table. Every table has its own CSV button.

Reports count approved entries only, so report totals and the ledger's closing balance for the same range always agree. If they ever disagree, something is wrong; say so.

Settings

Auditor · month close only Administrator · everything

Budget lines

Add a line, rename it, set a monthly budget, deactivate it. A line in use cannot be deleted — deactivate it instead, and it disappears from the entry form for new entries while past entries keep their history intact. Renaming is safe: the audit trail keeps the old name against the entries that used it.

People

Add someone, change their role, reset a password, deactivate an account when they leave. Deactivating is the right move for a departure — the person can no longer sign in, but everything they recorded stays attributed to them. You cannot demote or deactivate your own account; that is what stops the last administrator locking everyone out.

Close a month

  1. Pick the month.
  2. Enter the cash you physically counted, if you counted it.
  3. The screen shows the book balance, your counted figure and the variance between them, before you confirm.
  4. Confirm.

If anything in that month is still pending, closing is refused — clear the queue first. Closed months are listed underneath with a Reopen action for administrators, and reopening is logged like everything else.

07 The monthly routine

Every day
Accountant

Record entries as they happen, while you still have the receipt in your hand. Attach the reference or a photo link to every expense.

2–3× a week
Auditor

Clear the approvals queue. Entries sitting pending mean the balance on the Overview is understating what the hub has actually spent.

Month end
Auditor
  1. Clear the approvals queue completely.
  2. Open Exceptions and work through each list. Ask about anything you do not recognise.
  3. Reconcile against the outside record: the bank statement, the mobile money statement, and a physical count of the cash box.
  4. Open Settings → Close a month, enter the cash you counted, check the variance, confirm.
  5. Keep the CSV export from Reports for the month with your records.

Once the month is closed its numbers cannot drift. Anything discovered afterwards is recorded as a correction in the current month, which is exactly how a paper cashbook handles it.

09 Questions people ask

My entry does not show in the balance. Is it lost?

No. It is pending. Look at the Status column — it will say Pending, with a dash in the balance column. It counts the moment an auditor approves it.

Why can't I approve my own entry? I know it is correct.

Because the value of the check comes entirely from a second pair of eyes. An approval you gave yourself proves nothing to an auditor next year. The rule holds for administrators too.

Where is the delete button?

There isn't one. Void the entry instead, with a reason. Deleting would leave a hole in the record with nothing to explain it; a voided entry explains itself.

I recorded 45,000 but it was 40,000.

If it is still pending, edit it. If it has been approved, edit it anyway — it goes back to pending and an auditor reviews the change, seeing exactly what moved. Both versions stay in the audit trail.

Can an administrator quietly change the audit trail?

No. Nothing writes to the trail except the act of doing something, and there is no screen, setting or button anywhere that edits or removes an entry from it.

We closed the month and then found a receipt.

Record it in the current month with a description saying which month it belongs to. That is what a paper cashbook does. If it is large enough to matter to the closed month's figures, an administrator can reopen the month — and the reopening is logged.

Two people recorded the same payment.

It will appear under Exceptions → Possible duplicates. Void one of them with the reason "duplicate of the entry recorded by …".

Someone left the hub.

Deactivate their account in Settings → People. Do not delete it and do not reuse it for a new person — everything they recorded is attributed to them, and that attribution needs to stay true.

The numbers on Reports and the Ledger disagree.

They should never disagree for the same date range, since both count only approved entries. Check that you are comparing the same range and that the ledger is not filtered. If they still disagree, report it — that is a real defect.

10 What this system does not do

By design, so you know to handle these elsewhere:

  • It does not store receipt images. It stores a link to one.
  • It handles Rwandan francs only.
  • It is not double-entry bookkeeping and has no chart of accounts.
  • It does not issue invoices.
  • It does not connect to a bank or mobile money feed. Reconciliation is a human comparing this book against a statement.
  • It does not send email or SMS notifications. The pending count in the sidebar is the reminder.
